# 01 · See the map

**Step 1 of 7 · Phase: Awake · Output: the map of one workflow, antibodies drawn on.**

You map one workflow, not the enterprise. An enterprise map is a later output, once you have a working edge to justify it. Pick the workflow you suspect will be your first candidate; step 3 will confirm or overrule you, and a map of the wrong workflow still teaches you how to map.

The map is one map. Evolution sits on the axis; resistance sits on top as an annotation. The nodes on this map are the parts list that step 4 cuts, so be concrete. "Order handling" is not a component. "Manual re-keying of the intake form into the ERP" is.

## How to draw it

1. **User need at the top.** Who the workflow serves, and what they get from it. One line. If you cannot write it, you do not have a workflow, you have a department.
2. **Components down the chain.** Each thing the need depends on, and each thing that depends on. Steps, rules, approvals, fields, reports, meetings, roles, systems, integrations, and equally features, offers, price tiers, clauses, policies, and KPIs. Anything with a cost is a component.
3. **Place each component on the evolution axis:** genesis (novel, uncertain, nobody has done it before), custom (built for you, still changing), product (bought or rented, differentiated), commodity (utility, everyone has it, nobody competes on it).
4. **Mark, for each component, whether it is built or bought, and who consumes it.** A component nobody downstream consumes is a finding, not an oversight.
5. **Put a name on each component.** A person, not a function. This column is what the owner test in step 4 runs on, and filling it here is faster than filling it later.
6. **Draw the antibodies on it.** The antibodies are the processes, incentives, and habits that will restore every deleted part. They cluster where components move from custom to product, because that move is where roles, budgets, and identities are attached. Mark each one where it sits on the map. The twelve antibodies and a five-minute scoring are at [samuelpouyt.com/diagnostic](https://samuelpouyt.com/diagnostic).

No diagram tooling is required. An indented list or the table below is enough, and it has the advantage of being committable.

## The three questions the map must answer

1. **What is commodity but built custom?** Those are the first parts to delete or replace with something bought.
2. **What is genesis and stays human?** Those are the parts you protect, and where judgment lives. They become playbooks in step 5, never automation.
3. **What does nobody consume?** Those parts have no user need above them. They exist because someone, once, asked for them. Step 4 asks who.

If the map does not answer all three, it is not finished.

## Before you move on

- The map covers one workflow, from user need to the last thing it depends on.
- Every component has an evolution position, a built/bought mark, and a name.
- The antibodies are drawn where they sit, not listed in an appendix.
- The three questions are answered in writing.

Commit the map. Step 2 asks what all of this is for.
